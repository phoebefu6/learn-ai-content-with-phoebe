Part 1 · the content operating model

How content actually gets made here 15 min live

A content operation is not a pile of tools; it is a pipeline with owners. The AI-augmented shape is the same everywhere: idea, brief, draft, edit, QA, publish, repurpose. AI assists most of those stages and compresses the timeline; the human-edit gate is the point where generic slop is turned into something specific and true. Your job as a leader is to name the stages, place the gate, and give every step an owner.

LiveThe AI-augmented pipeline - stage by stage4 min▶

Read the pipeline left to right and notice where AI helps and where the human must stand. AI is genuinely strong at the front (idea, brief, draft) and the back (repurpose one piece into many). The middle - edit and QA - is where a person turns a fast, generic draft into something specific, true, and unmistakably yours. Skip the gate and you have simply automated the production of slop.

  • Idea - brief - draft. AI accelerates these; the creator still decides the angle and the point of view.
  • Edit (the gate). The non-negotiable human pass: cut filler, add real specifics, verify every fact, restore the voice.
  • QA - publish. A brand-and-rights check, then it ships - with disclosure where a4 said it is required.
  • Repurpose. One approved piece becomes many formats. This is where AI's leverage compounds - covered live in the creator track's b3.

Questions to ask your team

  • Draw our real pipeline - is it these seven stages, or do we have hidden hand-offs and re-work loops?
  • Where exactly is our human-edit gate, and is it ever skipped under deadline?
  • Which stages are we still doing by hand that AI could genuinely accelerate?
Self-studyThe roles around the pipeline3 min read▶

A pipeline without named owners quietly degrades. Four roles keep it healthy - and note that a "content brand GPT" is a role, not a person: a reusable, voice-loaded assistant that keeps drafts on-brand before a human ever touches them.

RoleOwns
CreatorsIdea, brief, and first draft - the angle and the point of view
EditorsThe human-edit gate: specifics, truth-check, voice, the ship decision
Content-ops ownerThe flow itself - stages, hand-offs, tooling, and cycle-time
Content brand GPTVoice + brief consistency at scale, so drafts start on-brand

Questions to ask your team

  • Do these four roles exist here, or are they collapsed into one overloaded person?
  • Who owns the flow end to end - and do they have the authority to fix a broken hand-off?
  • Would a shared content brand GPT reduce our re-work, and who would maintain it?
Part 2 · measuring content ROI

Prove it works without counting posts 15 min live

The easiest number to report is volume - and it is the one that means the least. AI makes output nearly free, so "we published more" proves nothing and can even signal a slop problem. Measure outcomes instead: engagement quality, conversions, reach that compounds, the leverage you get from reuse, and how fast a piece moves through the pipeline. Those are the numbers a business case can stand on.

LiveOutcomes, reuse, and cycle-time - not volume4 min▶

Three families of measure actually tell you the operation is working. Outcomes: is the content earning attention, trust, and action? Reuse leverage: how many quality formats come out of one idea? Cycle-time: how fast does a piece go from brief to published without cutting the edit gate? Raw output count is the vanity trap - it rewards exactly the behaviour that floods your channels with forgettable content.

Honest measure (track this)Vanity trap (do not reward)
Engagement quality - saves, replies, time spent, returning readersImpressions and raw view counts
Conversions - the action the content was forNumber of pieces published
Reach that compounds - content that keeps working over monthsPosts per week per creator
Reuse leverage - quality formats produced per ideaWord count or asset count shipped
Cycle-time - brief to published, edit gate intactSpeed gained by skipping the edit
The one-line ROI test "Did this content change what a reader felt, believed, or did - and can we still find it working next quarter?" If the only answer is "we made a lot of it", you are measuring the vanity column.

Questions to ask your team

  • Which column does our current content report live in - outcomes, or volume?
  • What is our reuse leverage today - how many formats do we actually get from one strong idea?
  • Do we know our cycle-time, and are we ever buying speed by skipping the edit gate?
Self-studyBuy vs build - specialized tools, a brand GPT, or a blend3 min read▶

Tooling is a business-case line, so decide it deliberately. There are three doors, and most mature operations end up at the third.

DoorGood whenWatch for
Buy specialized toolsYou need best-in-class image, video, or voice output fastCost stack, rights terms, fragmented voice across tools
Build a content brand GPTVoice consistency and reuse at scale matter mostMaintenance ownership, keeping it current, over-trust without the edit
Blend bothYou want on-brand drafting plus specialized productionIntegration effort; still one human-edit gate for everything

Whichever door you pick, the edit gate and the rights check from a4 are identical. Tools change the speed and the cost line; they never change who owns the voice and the claims.

Questions to ask your team

  • Which door are we on today - and did we choose it, or drift into it?
  • If we built a content brand GPT, who owns keeping it on-voice and current?
  • What is our real tooling spend, and is it buying outcomes or just output?
Part 3 · the business case

The one-pager that gets it funded 10 min live

A budget owner does not fund "we will use more AI". They fund a clear operation with a clear return. Your business case is short: the pipeline and quality bar you are asking to run, the tooling you need, the value you expect in outcome terms, the risks you are managing, and the specific ask. Keep it to a page and tie the value to the honest measures, never to volume.

LiveStructure the case - pipeline, value, risk, ask4 min▶

Five parts, in plain language, on one page:

  • The operation. The pipeline and the quality bar (a3) you are asking to run - with the human-edit gate named as non-negotiable.
  • The tooling ask. The buy / build / blend decision and its cost line, tied to what it enables.
  • Expected value. Outcomes and reuse leverage against a baseline - engagement quality, conversions, compounding reach, faster cycle-time. Not output count.
  • The risk. Slop and brand dilution if the edit gate is skipped; IP and disclosure exposure from a4. Name how the operation controls each.
  • The ask. The specific budget, roles, and mandate you need, and what you will report back.
Real world

The case that beat "just do more". A content lead was pushed to triple output on the same headcount. Instead of promising volume, she proposed a governed pipeline with an edit gate and a reuse target: fewer originals, each turned into many formats, measured on conversions and returning readers. The board funded it because the return was legible and the slop risk was named and owned - not because the number of posts went up.

This one-pager is the hand-off into a6, where the same operation becomes a 24-month strategy and the narrative that keeps it funded as the work turns agentic.

Questions to ask your team

  • What is our honest outcome baseline today - the number this case will be measured against?
  • Which risk is the budget owner most worried about, and how does our operation control it?
  • What is the smallest credible ask that still lets us prove the model?
Exercise

Sketch your pipeline + a mini business case ◐ 30-40 min total

  • Draw your real content pipeline - the actual stages, hand-offs, and where the human-edit gate sits (or should sit). Mark every stage where AI already helps.
  • Give each stage an owner from the four roles: creators, editors, content-ops owner, content brand GPT. Circle any stage with no clear owner - that is your first fix.
  • Pick one honest outcome baseline you can measure today (engagement quality, a conversion, returning readers, or reuse leverage). Write down the current number, even if it is rough.
  • Draft a one-page business case: the operation, the tooling ask (buy / build / blend), expected value against that baseline, the slop and IP risks and how you control them, and the specific ask.
  • Bring both to a6, where they become the 24-month roadmap and the funding narrative.
Questions to ask your team Where does our pipeline break under deadline? What one outcome would most convince our budget owner? And if AI made output free tomorrow, what would we choose to make less of - and better?

Check yourself

Three questions before you go 🎯 ◐ 90 seconds

1 · What does the content operating model include?

The operation is a pipeline with owners - idea, brief, draft, edit, QA, publish, repurpose - with the human-edit gate as the point where slop is turned into something true. Tools and targets are not the model.

2 · How should a content operation prove its ROI?

AI makes output nearly free, so volume proves nothing and can signal slop. Measure engagement quality, conversions, compounding reach, formats per idea, and cycle-time with the edit gate intact.

3 · On the buy-vs-build tooling decision...

Tools change speed and cost; they never change who owns the voice and the claims. Whichever door you pick, one human-edit gate and one rights check apply to everything.
