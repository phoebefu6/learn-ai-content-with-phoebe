Part 1 · the one tool this session gives you

Value vs risk: the scoring grid 6 min live

Score every AI content use-case on two axes. Value is what it buys you: speed, reach, and repurposing leverage. Risk is what it can cost you: slop and brand dilution, IP and likeness exposure, disclosure obligations, and factual error. Plot each use-case and the picture tells you what to do - top-left is a quick win to take now, top-right is high value but needs the rights cleared first, and anything that trades low value for high risk you simply avoid.

LiveReading the four quadrants3 min▶

The grid is not about being cautious or bold - it is about matching your appetite to the actual stakes of each use-case. Two questions decide everything: how much does this genuinely buy us, and what is the worst it could cost us. Score both honestly and the quadrant tells you the move.

  • Top-left - quick wins. High value, low risk: repurposing, and drafting that a human edits. Take these now; they compound speed with almost no exposure.
  • Top-right - clear the rights. High value, high risk: AI visuals, AI video and voice. Worth doing, but only after IP, likeness, and disclosure checks. We govern these fully in a4.
  • Bottom-left - low stakes. Low value, low risk: fine to experiment, but do not mistake motion for progress.
  • Bottom-right - avoid. Low value, high risk: autopublishing unedited content is the clearest example. The speed is not worth the slop and the exposure.
Questions to ask your team
  • For each thing we already do with AI, which quadrant is it really in - and did we ever score it, or just start?
  • Are we sitting in the low-stakes quadrant calling it innovation, while the real quick wins go untaken?
  • Is anything of ours in the avoid quadrant right now?
Self-studyScoring value and risk honestly2 min read▶

Give each axis a simple high/medium/low. On value, weigh speed (how much time it saves), reach (how many channels it feeds), and repurposing leverage (how far one brief travels). On risk, weigh slop and brand dilution, IP and likeness exposure, disclosure obligations, and factual error. The discipline is to score risk before you fall in love with the value - a use-case that saves a week is not a quick win if it puts a real person's likeness or a fabricated stat under your brand.

Questions to ask your team
  • When we score value, do we count reach and leverage, or just how fast it feels?
  • Who on the team is responsible for scoring the risk side, and do they have the authority to say no?

The content use-case inventory 5 min live

Here is the grid applied across the content work most teams actually do. Value and risk are typical, not fixed - your context can move any row - but the pattern holds: the safe leverage is in ideation, drafting, and repurposing, and the risk climbs sharply as you move into likeness, video, voice, and anything that publishes without a human.

That is exactly the order the creator track builds in, and the order you should adopt.

Real world

The rights bill nobody scored. A team shipped an AI-generated campaign visual that echoed a living illustrator's signature style. It looked great and cost nothing to make - until the complaint arrived. Pulling it, replacing it, and the legal review cost more than a year of the tool. The visual sat in the top-right quadrant the whole time; nobody scored the risk axis before publishing.

Self-studyWhy the risk axis exists at all2 min read▶

Value alone would tell you to do everything, fast. The risk axis is what keeps speed from becoming a liability, and it captures two very different dangers. The first is slop - brand dilution from publishing average content at volume, the quiet cost from a1. The second is exposure - IP and likeness of AI outputs, disclosure obligations, and fabricated facts, the sharp cost we govern in a4. A grid with only a value axis is how a team talks itself into the avoid quadrant.

Questions to ask your team
  • Do we treat slop and legal exposure as the same kind of risk, or price them separately?
  • Which row in this inventory would hurt us most if it went wrong - and have we protected it?
Part 3 · the order that earns trust

Sequencing: earn trust before you climb 4 min live

A scored grid tells you what is worth doing; sequencing tells you in what order. The rule is simple and it protects both your brand and your team's credibility: start where value is high and risk is low, keep the human edit, prove the quality bar holds, and only then move into the high-risk, high-value work with the rights and disclosure checks in place.

LiveThe order to adopt2 min▶
  • First - the quick wins. Ideation, repurposing, and drafting-to-edit. High value, low risk, and they build the team's fluency and your evidence that the quality bar works.
  • Always - keep the human edit. Every step above ships through a person. This is rail one from a1, and it is what makes the quick wins safe rather than just fast.
  • Then - the high-value, high-risk work. Visual, video, and voice, gated by the IP, likeness, and disclosure checks you set in a4. You move here because you earned the trust, not because the tool got tempting.
  • Never yet - unedited autopublish. That is era-three agentic work, and a1 was clear: it waits until the bar and the governance can run without a human in every loop.
Questions to ask your team
  • Have we taken the quick wins first, or did we chase the flashy high-risk use-case before the bar was proven?
  • What is the one signal that would tell us we have earned the right to climb to the next quadrant?

Exercise

Score five real use-cases and pick your first bet ◐ 25-35 min · decide tonight

The decision this session exists for. Not a hypothetical - your real content work, scored, so you leave with a first bet you can defend to your team and your leadership.

  • List five AI content use-cases your team is doing or seriously considering. Be specific: "repurpose the weekly article into five social posts", not "social".
  • Score each on value (high/medium/low across speed, reach, leverage) and risk (high/medium/low across slop, IP/likeness, disclosure, factual error).
  • Plot all five on the grid. Circle the quick wins in the top-left and flag anything sitting in the bottom-right avoid zone.
  • Pick your first bet: the highest-value, lowest-risk use-case you are not yet doing well. Write one sentence on why it, and one on how you will keep the human edit.
  • Note which one bet you most want to unlock later, and what check a4 would have to give you before you take it.
Questions to ask your team
  • Does everyone score the same use-case the same way - and if not, whose read on the risk axis wins?
  • What is stopping us from starting our first bet on Monday?
