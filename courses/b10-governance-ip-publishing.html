Part 1 · what scale actually exposes

Publishing at scale, safely: the risk stack 6 min live

At one post a week, a mistake is an incident. At volume, the same mistake is a pattern - and patterns are what get noticed. Five risks stack up the moment you publish AI content at scale. Read the stack bottom to top: lose the foundation and everything above it wobbles.

LiveThe five risks, and why volume makes them worse3 min▶

Each risk exists at any scale. Volume is the multiplier: it removes the pause where a human would have caught it, and it repeats the exposure until it is a habit rather than a slip.

  • Slop slipping through. At volume, the edit is the first thing dropped to hit targets. Unedited AI content floods the channel, distinctiveness dies, and the audience learns to scroll past you.
  • IP + likeness exposure. Loudest risk in this stack. AI text can echo copyrighted work; AI images and video can reproduce a living artist's style, a real face, or a recognizable voice. Every format carries it - text, image, video, and audio alike.
  • Undisclosed AI content. Where disclosure is required - AI-generated endorsements and certain content under FTC guidance - failing to disclose is a real exposure, not a nicety.
  • Missing provenance. No content credentials, no record of what was AI-made or edited. As authenticity practices become table stakes, no trail is its own liability.
  • Losing the single source of truth. The voice guide and the brief are the foundation. At volume, if they drift or get bypassed, every piece drifts with them.
The scale rule A control that depends on someone remembering does not survive volume. Every risk in this stack needs a gate that runs by default - a checklist, a spec, a required sign-off - not good intentions.
Self-studyWhy IP and disclosure sit loudest here2 min read▶

Slop is a reputation risk - slow, cumulative, recoverable. IP, likeness, and disclosure are different: they are legal and regulatory exposure, and a single published piece can trigger them. AI-generated text, images, video, and voice all carry copyright and training-data questions; using a real person's face or voice raises likeness and right-of-publicity issues; and undisclosed AI endorsements can breach FTC rules. That is why this session makes them loud - and why the controls that follow treat clearance and disclosure as required gates, not optional polish.

  • Inputs and outputs both. Do not feed others' IP or a real likeness in; and check what comes out, because AI can reproduce a style or a face you did not intend.
  • Disclosure is a rule, not a courtesy. Where AI content or endorsements require disclosure, treat it as mandatory and build it into the workflow.

Self-studyClearance and disclosure, in practice2 min read▶

Two controls do the heavy lifting, so make them concrete. IP clearance is a two-sided check: inputs (never feed others' copyrighted work or a real likeness without rights) and outputs (review AI images, video, and voice for a style, face, or voice that echoes a real person, and check the tool's commercial-use terms). Disclosure is a written policy: decide which content is AI-generated in a way that requires a label under FTC guidance, where the disclosure goes, and the exact words - then apply it every time, not case by case.

  • Clearance is inputs and outputs. Both sides fail loudly; check both before anything ships.
  • Disclosure is written, not judged live. A policy applied consistently beats a call made in a rush.
  • Provenance is the receipt. Content credentials and a record of what was AI-made are becoming table stakes - start the trail now.
Part 3 · who says it ships

The publish checklist + sign-off 4 min live

Controls only work if someone owns the gate. The publish checklist turns the risk stack into a short set of yes/no questions, and sign-off names who confirms them before a piece goes out. Ties straight back to leader session a4, from the creator's seat.

LiveWho signs off, and the correction plan2 min▶

Sign-off scales by tiering it. Routine content gets a light gate; sensitive content gets more eyes. The point is that every piece has a named owner for the checklist, and that no piece publishes with an open item.

  • Creator signs the craft. Edited, on-voice, distinctive, first line earns the read - the b9 gate, owned by the person who made it.
  • Editor signs the facts + fit. Every [verify] resolved, claims checked, on-brand against the source of truth.
  • Brand or legal signs the sensitive. Anything with real likeness, others' IP, endorsements, or regulated claims gets a brand or legal review before publish - not everything, but always the sensitive.
  • A correction + takedown plan. Decide in advance how you correct a published error and how fast you can take content down. At volume, "what do we do when one slips" is a when, not an if.
Real world

The image nobody cleared. A busy team published an AI-generated hero image that closely echoed a living illustrator's signature style. No clearance step existed, so no one caught it; the artist did. A one-line control - "AI images get an output review + a commercial-terms check before publish" - would have stopped it at the gate. The takedown was public; the checklist would have been quiet.

Self-studyTiering sign-off so it survives volume2 min read▶

The failure mode is a sign-off so heavy that people route around it to hit deadlines. So tier it. Most content is low-risk and needs only the creator's own gate plus the standard checklist. A smaller slice - real people, others' IP, endorsements, regulated or sensitive claims - gets an extra editor or brand/legal pass. Match the weight of the gate to the risk of the piece, and the controls stay used instead of bypassed. Ties back to a4: governance that is proportional is governance that actually runs.

Demo 1 of 2

Run a publish-readiness checklist on a Cadence piece ★ 13 min · everyone builds

Take one Cadence piece you built earlier in the track - a post, an article, a visual, a video script - and run it through the publish gate for real. This is the finale in one exercise: prove the engine can ship without shipping a problem.

Pick one real piece. Something you actually made in b4-b8, ideally with an AI image or an external reference in it so the IP checks bite.

Edited? Confirm the b9 anti-slop pass ran - cliches cut, specifics in, POV sharp, voice on. If not, run it now. No unedited piece passes.

Facts verified? Check every claim, stat, quote, and date. No live [verify] tags. If you cannot verify it, cut it or replace it.

IP cleared? Inputs rights-clear, outputs reviewed for a style/face/voice that echoes a real person, tool commercial terms checked. Disclosed where AI use requires it.

On-voice + signed? Measure it against the brand GPT source of truth, then apply the right sign-off tier. Only when every box is yes does it publish.

★ The publish-readiness prompt (you still make every call)Here is a piece I am about to publish: [paste, or describe the visual/video]. Run a publish-readiness review as a yes/no checklist: 1) Edited - anti-slop pass done (cliches cut, real specifics, POV, on-voice)? 2) Facts - every claim, stat, quote, date verified; no open [verify]? 3) IP - inputs rights-clear, outputs reviewed for likeness/style, tool terms ok? 4) Disclosure - AI use disclosed where required? 5) Source of truth - on-brand against our voice + brief? List what fails and why. I will resolve each item before publishing.
The gate is the deliverable If the piece passes, you have proof the engine is safe to run. If it fails, you just caught - quietly, at the gate - something that would otherwise have been a public correction. Both outcomes are the win.
Demo 2 of 2

Write Cadence's disclosure + IP-clearance spec ★ 8 min · build your own

Turn the two loudest controls into a written spec the whole team runs. Not a philosophy - a one-page document that says exactly what to disclose, what to clear, and who signs off. You draft it with AI, then edit it to your reality.

Give AI the two rails and your context: the formats you publish, which use real people or external references, and who is on the team (creator, editor, brand/legal).

Ask for two sections. Disclosure: what content must be labelled as AI, where the label goes, and the exact wording, tied to FTC guidance. IP clearance: the input rules and the output review for text, image, video, and voice, plus the tool-terms check.

Add the sign-off tiers: routine (creator + checklist), standard (+ editor), sensitive (+ brand/legal). Name who owns each gate.

Edit it hard to your reality, add a correction + takedown line, and adopt it. This is the spec that makes the whole engine safe to run at scale.

Homework

Try it yourself - this week ◐ 30-45 min total

  • Run the publish-readiness checklist on three real pieces before they go out this week. Note which gate catches the most - that is where your risk actually lives.
