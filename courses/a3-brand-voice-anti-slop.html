Part 1 · the standard that keeps a brand distinctive at speed

The anti-slop standard 6 min live

AI floods every channel with content that is fluent, confident, and instantly forgettable - slop. Because fluency is now free, it has stopped being a signal of quality or of you. The differentiator is not the model; it is three things you own: a documented voice, a real quality bar, and a human edit that is mandatory, not optional. Consistency at scale needs the voice written down so it can feed a content brand GPT. And distinctiveness, not volume, is what actually earns the reader.

LiveFluent is free now - so it stops being the bar3 min▶

For years, grammatical, structured, confident writing was a rough proxy for effort and quality. AI has made all of that free, which means it no longer signals anything - least of all that the content came from you. If your only bar is "reads fine", every competitor clears it in seconds, and your audience learns to scroll past content that is technically correct and completely forgettable. The anti-slop standard replaces "reads fine" with something the model cannot fake on its own.

  • Slop is an averageness problem, not a typo problem. The draft is clean and says nothing. That is exactly what floods everyone's feed.
  • Three things it cannot supply. Your documented voice, a real point of view, and verified facts. Those are the standard.
  • The human edit is where the value moved. AI gets to a generic 70 percent fast; the specific, true, on-brand 30 percent is human, and it is mandatory.
Real world

The brand that published its way into silence. A team tripled its output overnight with AI. Six months later engagement had cratered - not because the content was wrong, but because it was indistinguishable from everyone else's. They cut volume in half, edited hard, and added a real point of view. Reach recovered. Speed had been the easy part; the standard was the job.

Questions to ask your team

  • If we removed our logo, could a reader tell our content from a competitor's? Honestly?
  • What is our bar for publishing right now - is it written down, or is it "it reads fine"?
  • Where have we already started trading distinctiveness for volume?
Self-studyThe gate: what has to clear before we publish2 min read▶

The gate in the diagram is the standard made concrete. Nothing publishes until it has passed each step - and the last step, a human sign-off, is the one that is never skipped for speed. Print it and put it where drafts get reviewed.

  • Voice check - does this sound like us, measured against our documented voice, not like generic AItext?
  • Specifics + point of view - is there real detail and a genuine perspective, or is it interchangeable filler?
  • Fact-check - is every stat, quote, and claim verified? AI invents confident numbers that do not exist.
  • Human sign-off - has a named person read it and taken ownership of what ships under our name?

The gate is cheap to run and far cheaper than the reputation it protects. It is also the same move your creators practise hands-on in the anti-slop pass (b9).

Questions to ask your team

  • Which of these four gates do we actually run today, and which get skipped when we are busy?
  • Who is the named human sign-off for each channel?
Part 2 · making the standard hold across a whole team

Voice and quality at scale 6 min live

A standard in one editor's head does not scale. To hold a voice across many people and many pieces, four things have to exist on paper: a documented brand voice with sample copy and a "never say" list, a quality checklist, a human-edit-required rule, and a habit of measuring distinctiveness rather than volume. Written down, the voice can also feed a content brand GPT - which is how you get consistency at scale without one person becoming the bottleneck.

LiveDocument the voice so it can travel3 min▶

A brand voice that lives only in one person's judgment cannot be applied by ten people or by a model. Documenting it makes it teachable and, crucially, feedable - a written voice becomes the system prompt for a content brand GPT, so every drafter starts on-brand instead of beige. Keep it short and concrete.

  • Sample copy. A few short passages that sound unmistakably like you - more useful than adjectives like "friendly" or "bold".
  • A "never say" list. The cliches, the corporate filler, the phrases that are not you. This is what stops slop faster than anything.
  • Do / do not pairs. The same sentence written on-brand and off-brand, so the difference is visible, not abstract.
The "never say" list earns its keep first Teams argue for weeks about what their voice is. A "never say" list is faster to agree on and instantly useful - it catches the beige phrasing before it ships, and it is the easiest thing to feed a content brand GPT.

Questions to ask your team

  • Do we have our voice written down, or does it live in one editor's head?
  • What are the ten phrases that should be on our "never say" list?

  • If we built a content brand GPT tomorrow, what would we feed it as our voice?
Part 3 · the part only the leader can do

The leader sets the bar 4 min live

Tools do not set standards; leaders do. What you are willing to publish becomes the brand - not what you say the brand should be. The single biggest risk is a gap between the "quality matters" message and what actually ships when a deadline is close. Close that gap and the standard holds; leave it open and the team learns that the real rule is "ship it".

LiveWhat you tolerate becomes your brand2 min▶

Every piece you let through sets the floor. If a generic, unedited draft ships once because the deadline was tight, that becomes the new normal, and the standard erodes one exception at a time. The brand is not the deck of values; it is the average of what you actually publish. Protecting the bar is unglamorous and it is the whole job.

Real world

The one "just this once" that reset the floor. A team held a strong edit standard until a launch week when an unedited draft went out to hit the date. It performed fine, so it happened again. Within a quarter the edit had quietly become optional, and the content had drifted back to the average. The leader had not changed the policy - they had changed what they tolerated, and the team read that correctly.

Questions to ask your team

  • What have we let ship recently that did not clear our own bar? Why did we let it?
  • When a deadline and the standard collide, which one wins here - really?
LiveSay quality matters, then protect the edit2 min▶

The message "quality matters" is free; protecting the edit costs time, money, and the discomfort of holding a deadline. Your team watches what you do under pressure, not what you say in the all-hands. Consistency between the two is what makes the standard believable. That means funding the edit, defending the time it takes, and praising the distinctive piece over the fast one.

  • Fund the edit. If the human edit is mandatory, staff and schedule for it. An unfunded rule is a wish.
  • Reward the right thing. Celebrate the piece that only your team could have made, not the one that shipped fastest.
  • Model it yourself. Send your own AI-drafted content back for an edit. The standard is real when it applies to you too.

Questions to ask your team

  • Does our calendar and staffing actually make room for the edit, or is it assumed to be free?
  • What did we celebrate last month - the distinctive piece, or the fast one?
Your turn

Draft your anti-slop quality bar and voice one-pager ◐ 30-45 min total

A short artifact you can circulate this week: a one-page standard your team can point to. Draft it with your leads, then edit it hard - which is the standard in miniature.

  • Write your quality bar as a short gate: voice check, specifics + point of view, fact-check, human sign-off. Keep it to a single page anyone can hold.
  • Start your documented voice - three passages of sample copy that sound like you, and a first "never say" list of ten phrases.
  • State the non-negotiable in one line: nothing publishes without a human edit and a fact-check. Name who owns sign-off for each channel.
  • Decide the one distinctiveness signal you will watch (saves, replies, "is this unmistakably us?") instead of volume.
  • Circulate the one-pager to the team and book 20 minutes to pressure-test it together.

Questions to ask your team

  • Would this one-pager actually change a decision under deadline, or is it decoration?
  • What is one phrase on the "never say" list we will all agree to catch, starting today?
  • Who is accountable for the standard when I am not in the room?

Three questions before you go 🎯 ◐ 90 seconds

1 · What is the heart of the anti-slop standard?

Fluency is free now, so it is not the bar. The standard is a voice you can point to, a quality gate, and a human edit that is never skipped for speed.

2 · Why document the brand voice instead of leaving it in an editor's head?

Voice in one head does not scale. Written down - with sample copy and a "never say" list - it becomes teachable and feedable, keeping content on-brand across the whole team.
