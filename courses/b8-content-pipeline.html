Part 1 · covers brief-to-publish as a repeatable flow

The pipeline: brief to publish, on repeat 6 min live

A content pipeline is a fixed sequence of stages every piece moves through, with clear gates where quality is checked. The brand GPT drives consistency across the stages; the human edit and the QA check are the control points that stop bad work from reaching the audience. Same flow, every piece - that is what makes output both fast and reliable.

LiveSix stages, two gates - walk the flow3 min▶

Every piece moves left to right through the same stations. The stations do not change; only the piece does. The two lime stations are gates - nothing passes them without a human sign-off - which is exactly what keeps the speed from turning into slop.

  • Brief -> Draft. The documented brief (from b2) goes into the brand GPT; it returns an on-system first pass instead of a blank-page guess.
  • Edit (gate 1). The human edit - specifics, point of view, voice. Nothing skips this. This is the value, not a formality.
  • QA (gate 2). Facts verified, claims checked, rights and disclosure confirmed. The last stop before the audience.
  • Publish -> Repurpose. Ship it, then feed it back into the b3 repurposing move - one idea becomes many. The pipeline loops.
★ Try it now (any chat AI) - name your gatesHere are my content stages: brief, draft, edit, QA, publish, repurpose. For the edit and QA gates, write me a 3-item pass/fail checklist each - what must be true before a piece moves on. Keep it blunt and specific to publishing.
Real world

The team that added one gate and got its quality back. A content team was shipping AI drafts straight to publish to hit volume - and slowly becoming noise. They added exactly one station to the flow: a required human edit before anything published. Nothing else changed. Output stayed high, quality returned, and the gate cost less than the reputation it protected.

Self-studyThe pipeline as a one-page standard2 min read▶

Write the flow down once and it becomes a standard anyone can follow. That is the whole point of a pipeline - it survives you being on holiday.

  • Brief: the documented ask - idea, audience, format, angle, must-haves. No brief, no draft.
  • Draft: brand GPT produces the on-system first pass from the brief.
  • Edit: the required human pass - specific, true, unmistakably yours.
  • QA: facts, claims, rights, disclosure - all confirmed.
  • Publish: to the channel, in the format's template.
  • Repurpose: one idea into the next formats; the loop restarts.
Part 2 · the engine that keeps drafts on-system

The content brand GPT + reusable templates 6 min live

Consistency at scale does not come from remembering to paste your voice guide into every chat. It comes from loading your documented voice, brief format, and edit rules into a custom GPT or a saved instruction set once, so every draft starts on-system. Add a reusable template per format and the batching that follows, and the pipeline runs on rails instead of willpower.

LiveLoad the voice once, reuse it everywhere3 min▶

A content brand GPT is just your standards, written down and loaded into the tool so you do not re-explain them every time. Voice from your documented guide, the brief format from b2, and the edit rules from your anti-slop pass - all in the system instructions. Then a template per format holds the shape, and batching lets you run many pieces through the same setup in one sitting.

  • Load it once. Voice, brief format, and edit rules in the instructions - drafts come out on-system, not generic.
  • Template per format. A reusable skeleton for the article, the post, the short-video script - the shape is decided, you fill the specifics.
  • Batch it. Run several briefs through the same GPT and templates in one session; the human edit still happens per piece.
The brand GPT does not replace the edit A loaded GPT gets you a better, more on-brand 70%. The human edit gate is still non-negotiable - the GPT makes the draft start closer to you, it does not make it finished.

Part 3 · who does what, and where AI slots in

Roles + handoffs 4 min live

A pipeline is also a set of handoffs between people. Even a team of one benefits from naming the roles, because it makes clear where AI helps and where a human must sign off. AI slots into the drafting and repurposing stages; it never removes the human at the edit and QA gates.

LiveBrief, draft, edit, QA, publish - who owns each2 min▶

Assign an owner to every stage, even if the same person wears several hats. The owner is accountable for the gate being met before the handoff. AI is a tool inside a stage, not an owner of one - it drafts and repurposes, humans edit and sign off.

  • Briefer owns the ask - a clear, documented brief before anything starts.
  • Drafter (with the brand GPT) produces the on-system first pass.
  • Editor owns gate 1 - the human edit that makes it specific, true, and yours.
  • QA owns gate 2 - facts, claims, rights, disclosure confirmed.
  • Publisher ships to the channel and kicks off repurposing.
Where AI slots in without removing the human AI drafts and repurposes; it can suggest at every stage. But the edit and QA gates are owned by people - that is the line that keeps the operation from becoming an unedited slop feed.
Demo 1 of 2

Build Cadence's content brand GPT + run one idea through ★ 13 min · everyone builds

Assemble the engine, then prove it works. You will load Cadence's voice, brief format, and edit rules into a custom GPT or a saved instruction block, then run a single idea from brief all the way to a publish-ready, edited draft.

Gather Cadence's standards you already documented: the voice notes, the b2 brief format, and your edit rules. Paste them into a custom GPT's instructions, or into one reusable instruction block at the top of a chat.

Write one real brief in your format - idea, audience, format, angle, must-haves - and hand it to the loaded GPT.

Take the on-system draft it returns. Notice it starts closer to Cadence's voice than a blank chat would - that is the load working.

Run gate 1 by hand: edit for specifics, a real point of view, and voice. Then gate 2: verify facts, check claims, confirm rights and disclosure.

You now have a publish-ready piece and a repeatable setup. Save the GPT's instructions - that is the reusable engine, not this one piece.

★ The content brand GPT instructions (load once)You are Cadence's content assistant. Draft everything on-system using the rules below. VOICE: [paste the documented voice - tone, do's, don'ts, example lines]. BRIEF FORMAT: expect idea, audience, format, angle, must-haves; ask for any that are missing. EDIT RULES: no fabricated facts, stats, or quotes; specifics over filler; distinctiveness over volume; sound like Cadence, not like generic AI. When I give you a brief, return a first draft in the requested format, flag anything I must verify, and never invent numbers or quotes. I will edit and QA before publishing.
Real world

The setup that made a new hire productive on day one. A team's quality lived in one editor's head. They wrote the voice and rules into a shared brand GPT and a one-page pipeline. The next hire produced on-brand drafts in their first week - not because they were experienced, but because the system carried the standard. The operation, not the individual, held the quality.

Demo 2 of 2

A reusable template + a QA checklist for one format ★ 8 min · build your own

Turn one format into rails. You will make a reusable skeleton for a single format and the QA checklist that guards its publish gate - two artifacts you keep and reuse every time that format comes up.

Pick one format you publish often - a LinkedIn post, a newsletter, a short-video script.

Ask the brand GPT for a reusable template for that format: the fixed sections, what goes in each, and the length. This is the skeleton, not the content.

Ask it for a QA checklist for that format - the pass/fail items to confirm before publish: facts verified, claims checked, rights and disclosure, on-brand voice, CTA present.

Edit both to Cadence's reality. Save them next to the brand GPT so the whole format runs on rails - fill the template, pass the checklist, publish.

★ The template + QA-checklist promptFor a [format, e.g. LinkedIn post], produce two reusable artifacts I will keep: 1) A TEMPLATE - the fixed sections in order, one line on what goes in each, and the target length. Skeleton only, no example content. 2) A QA CHECKLIST - the pass/fail items to confirm before publishing: facts verified, claims checked, rights + disclosure done, on-brand voice, one clear CTA. Keep both short enough to reuse every time. I will edit them to our standards.
Templates make batching safe Once a format has a template and a QA checklist, you can run several pieces through it in one sitting and still catch the same things every time. Batching without a checklist is how volume outruns quality.
