Part 1 · the craft canon on hooks and short-form

The hook is the whole battle 6 min live

On a social feed nobody owes you the second line. The first line either stops the scroll or the rest of your work is never read. That is good news for AI use: a model can generate twenty hook options in seconds, which means you can afford to be picky - reject the eighteen that could open anyone's post and keep the two that carry a real angle only you would take. AI supplies volume of options; you supply the judgment about which one earns the payoff.

LiveGenerate many, keep the one with an angle3 min▶

The mistake is asking AI for "a good hook" and taking the first one. The move is asking for many, then editing ruthlessly. Most will be beige - "Here is what nobody tells you about..." could open any post on earth. A few will hit a real tension, a surprising number, a specific moment. Those are the keepers, and even those you sharpen by hand.

  • Ask for volume. Ten to twenty hooks costs seconds. Options are free; pick like they are not.
  • Reject the generic. If a hook could open anyone's post, cut it. Distinctiveness is the whole test.
  • Sharpen the survivors. Add the real number, the specific name, the honest admission. The angle is yours, not the model's.
★ Try it now (any chat AI) - hook volumeGive me 15 first-line hooks for a short post on [topic]. Vary the angle: a surprising number, a contrarian take, a specific moment, an honest admission, a question. No "in today's world", no "here is what nobody tells you". One line each.
Real world

Nineteen thrown away, one that worked. A creator generated twenty hooks for a post, deleted eighteen on sight, and kept two with a real angle. The one they posted opened with a specific number from their own week. It outperformed a month of "value-packed tips" posts - not because AI wrote a better line, but because a human picked the one line that was actually theirs.

Self-studyWhat makes a hook stop the scroll2 min read▶
  • Tension over summary. A hook that sets up a gap ("I was wrong about X") beats one that pre-announces the answer.
  • Specific over vague. "3 posts, 40 hours, zero replies" stops more scrolls than "consistency is hard".
  • Honest over hyped. Real admissions read as human; "game-changer" and "unlock" read as slop.
  • One promise, kept. The hook makes one promise; the payoff delivers exactly that. A hook the body does not honor is bait, and the feed punishes bait.

You will use these as the pick criteria in Demo 1 - AI generates the field, this list is how you judge it.

Part 2 · native formats and the human check

Native + human, at volume 5 min live

Short-form is not one thing. A post, a thread, and a caption each have their own shape, and content that ignores the native format reads as reposted filler. Volume tempts you to paste the same block everywhere; resist it. And whatever the format, one human check runs last: cut the engagement-bait and the AI tells, so it sounds like a person, not a content machine.

FormatWhat fits itThe human check
Single postOne idea, one angle, a clean close - short and self-containedCut the "agree?" bait; keep one honest line only you would write
ThreadOne argument across steps, each line a reason to keep goingNo padding tweets; every step earns its place or gets cut
CaptionSets up the visual, adds context the image cannot carryNot a description of the picture - say the thing the image cannot
Reply / commentSpecific reaction, adds one idea, not a link dropSounds like you in a conversation, not a broadcast
LiveThe AI tells that get content scrolled past3 min▶

Audiences have learned the smell of AI social copy, and it costs you reach. The tells are predictable, which means they are easy to cut on the last pass. This is rail one applied to short-form: AI drafts the volume, the human edit is what makes it publishable.

  • Engagement bait. "Thoughts?", "Agree?", "Drop a 🔥" - manufactured interaction. Cut it; let a real point invite a real reply.
  • The tidy triad. Everything in threes, every line the same length, emoji bullets down the side. Break the rhythm so it reads human.
  • Beige confidence. "This will transform how you...", "The secret to..." - big claims, no specifics. Replace with one real detail.
The last-line human check Before posting, read it aloud. If a sentence sounds like a brand account or a LinkedIn generator, rewrite it as something you would actually say. One human line beats ten polished ones.
Self-studyBatching a week without sounding the same2 min read▶
  • Batch the making, vary the angle. Producing a week in one sitting is efficient; posting seven versions of the same sentence is not. Consistency is the theme; sameness is the failure.
  • One idea, many formats. The b3 move applies here - a single idea becomes a post, a thread, and a caption, each native, not copy-pasted.
  • Keep a hook bank. The rejected-but-decent hooks from Demo 1 are not waste - park them for next week.

Batching is how creators stay consistent without burning out. The discipline is editing each piece to its format and to sound human, even when you made ten at once.

Part 3 · the batch every posting week assumes

From one idea to a batch 4 min live

You already have Cadence's core idea and brief from b2. Short-form at scale is not "invent something new every day" - it is "take one strong idea and express it across a week, natively." That is the sustainable way to stay present in the feed without the quality falling off a cliff by Thursday.

LiveConsistency without sameness2 min▶

Take the Cadence idea and ask AI to help you find the different angles inside it - the objection, the surprising data point, the behind-the-scenes moment, the honest lesson. Each angle becomes a native piece. The week hangs together because it is one idea; it does not bore because each piece enters from a different door.

Real world

One idea, five doors. A creator took a single insight and posted it five ways in a week - a stat post, a story thread, a contrarian take, a caption on a screenshot, and a short reply-turned-post. Followers saw a consistent theme, not a broken record. Reach compounded because the idea was familiar and the angle was always fresh.

Self-studyThe batch beats the daily scramble2 min read▶
  • Decide the idea once. Reuse the b2 brief - audience, angle, and the one thing worth saying. AI riffs off a strong brief far better than off a blank page.
  • Draft the week in one sitting. Momentum keeps the voice consistent and frees the rest of the week for engagement, not scrambling.
  • Edit each piece to its format. The batch is the draft; the native edit and the human check are what ship. This feeds the pipeline you build in b8.
Demo 1 of 2

Hooks to a post and a thread ★ 13 min · everyone builds

Generate the field of hooks, pick the three with a real angle, sharpen them, then build a finished post and a thread from the best one. This is the whole short-form move in one sitting.

Take one Cadence idea from your b2 brief. Ask any chat AI for 10 hook options across varied angles - surprising number, contrarian take, honest admission, specific moment, question.

Judge the field against Part 1: cut every hook that could open anyone's post. Keep the 3 that carry a real angle.

Sharpen those 3 by hand - add a real number, a specific name, or an honest detail from your own experience. Now they are yours, not the model's.

Pick the strongest. Draft a single post from it: hook, payoff that keeps the promise, one clean close - no engagement bait.

Draft a thread from the same angle: each line a reason to keep reading, no padding. Then run the last-line human check on both.

The lazy ask - one generic hook (do not use)Write me a good hook for a post about our product. Make it engaging and add a question at the end so people comment.
★ The hook-field prompt (then pick + sharpen by hand)Here is my idea and audience: [paste b2 brief]. Give me 10 first-line hooks, one line each, varied by angle: - a surprising number, a contrarian take, an honest admission, a specific moment, a question. Rules: no "in today's world", no "here is what nobody tells you", no engagement bait. Then I will pick 3, sharpen them with real specifics, and build a post + a thread myself.
Why generate ten to keep one The value is not the model writing a great hook - it is you having enough options to reject the generic. Ten cheap hooks so you can afford to keep only the one that is truly yours. That is the anti-slop trade in miniature.
Demo 2 of 2

Batch a week of native short-form ★ 8 min · build your own

One idea, a week of content, three native formats - then edited so it sounds like a person, not a scheduler. This is how you stay consistent without going stale.

Give the AI one Cadence idea and ask it to surface 5 distinct angles inside it - objection, data point, behind-the-scenes, honest lesson, contrarian take.

Assign formats: turn one angle into a single post, one into a short thread, one into a caption for a visual. Each native to its shape, not copy-pasted.

Edit each piece to sound human: cut the bait, break the tidy triad, add one line only you would write. Read each aloud.

Park the two unused angles in a hook bank for next week. You just built a batch and a backlog in one sitting - the input to your b8 pipeline.

★ The week-batch prompt (then edit each to human)Here is one idea + audience: [paste]. Find 5 distinct angles inside this single idea (objection, surprising data point, behind-the-scenes, honest lesson, contrarian take). Then draft: - 1 single post from angle A (hook, payoff, clean close, no bait) - 1 short thread from angle B (each line earns the next) - 1 caption from angle C (says what a visual cannot) Keep the same voice across all three. I will edit each to sound human before posting.
Real world

A week in an hour, edited to sound like a person. A solo creator batched a full week of short-form from one idea in about an hour, then spent the real effort on the human edit - cutting bait, adding one specific line to each. The week read as consistent and alive, not automated. The batch bought back the time; the edit kept the audience.

Homework

Try it yourself - this week ◐ 30-45 min total

  • Finish both demos if you did not complete them live - especially the hook field. Generating ten to keep one is the habit to build.
  • Post one piece from Demo 1 for real and watch whether the first line held the scroll. The hook is the test.
  • Run the week-batch prompt on a real Cadence idea and edit each piece to sound human. Keep the two spare angles in a hook bank.
  • Save your three sharpest hooks from this week. Those become your reusable hook patterns.
  • Optional: screenshot one post of yours that reads as AI slop - the bait, the triad, the beige confidence - as a reminder of what the human check must catch.
